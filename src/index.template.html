Vanadzor

The city on stage

Vanadzor is the third-largest city in Armenia and the capital of Lori Province, about 128 km north of Yerevan. It sits at roughly 1,350 m in the valley where the Tandzut and Vanadzor rivers run into the Pambak, hemmed in on either side by the Bazum and Pambak ranges, both of which rise above 2,500 m. That valley floor is why the city is long and narrow rather than round, and why its districts are strung out along the water.

It was known for centuries as Gharakilisa, was renamed Kirovakan in 1935 after the Soviet official Sergey Kirov, and took the name Vanadzor on 25 June 1992, after independence. Under the Soviet Union it was a chemical-industry town — the acetate silk plant was the largest single employer — and much of that industry did not survive the transition. The infamous 1988 Spitak earthquake struck the city hard — roughly half its building stock was damaged or destroyed — though it shook less violently than Spitak and Gyumri, which sat closer to the epicentre and lost far more.
